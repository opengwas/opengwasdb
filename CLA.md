# Contributor License Agreements

opengwasdb is released under the GNU General Public License v3 (`GPL-3.0-only`). The University of Bristol holds the copyright, and keeps the option to offer opengwasdb under other licences as well (dual licensing). To make that possible, every contribution from outside the University of Bristol needs a Contributor License Agreement (CLA) before it can be merged.

We use the [Harmony](https://www.harmonyagreements.org/) agreements, version 1.0, with the "Any" outbound licence option:

| You are contributing | Agreement |
|---|---|
| as an individual, on your own behalf | [HA-CLA-I-Any](cla/HA-CLA-I-Any.md), the Harmony Individual Contributor License Agreement |
| on behalf of a company, university or other organisation (including work you do as an employee) | [HA-CLA-E-Any](cla/HA-CLA-E-Any.md), the Harmony Entity Contributor License Agreement |

In short:
- **You keep the copyright** in your contribution.
- You give the University of Bristol a broad licence to use it, including a patent licence.
- The University may license your contribution under any licence, as long as it also stays available under the licence opengwasdb uses on the date you contributed (GPLv3).
- Non-software material (documentation, images) may also be licensed under CC BY 4.0.
- The agreements are governed by the laws of England and Wales.

Read the full agreement before you sign. It is a legally binding document.

## How to sign

### Individuals

When you open your first pull request, the CLA Assistant bot will comment on it. Read [HA-CLA-I-Any](cla/HA-CLA-I-Any.md), then reply on the pull request with exactly:

```text
I have read the CLA Document and I hereby sign the CLA
```

Your GitHub username, the date and the pull request are recorded on the `cla-signatures` branch of this repository. You sign once; it covers all your later contributions.

If you are employed, and your employer may own what you write (for example, if you write it as part of your job), your employer must approve your signature, or sign the Entity agreement (section 3(c) of the agreement).

### Organisations

1. Open an issue titled `Entity CLA: <organisation name>`. Do not put signed documents or personal addresses in the issue.
2. A maintainer will arrange for an authorised signatory to sign [HA-CLA-E-Any](cla/HA-CLA-E-Any.md), and will countersign it for the University of Bristol.
3. Then the maintainers add the GitHub usernames of the contributors the organisation authorises to the CLA workflow's allow-list.

## Work you do not own

If your contribution includes work you do not own in full (for example, code copied from another project, or written with someone else), do not submit that part as your own. Instead:
- name the work, its author and its licence in the pull request description;
- keep its original copyright and licence notices;
- submit only material whose licence is compatible with GPLv3.

The maintainers will decide whether it can be included.

## University of Bristol staff

Software written by University of Bristol staff in the course of their employment is normally owned by the University under its intellectual property policy, so it needs no CLA. If you are unsure whether this applies to you, sign the individual agreement anyway. The maintainers keep those contributors on the CLA workflow's allow-list.
